## 5. What genuinely works

Stated as plainly as the failures, because a decision needs both.

- **CI is healthy.** 27 of the last 30 runs on `main` green. The 3 failures were
  the wall-clock drift in the decisions projection, diagnosed and fixed today.
- **539 unit tests pass**, and the `t check` preflight covers fmt, coordinate
  audit, capability schema, route resolution, signatures, projections,
  reconciliation and submodule pins.
- **The honesty machinery is real and load-bearing.** A missing
  `implementation_status` resolves to `unlabeled`, never silently `aspirational`
  — with a regression test. The projections gate refuses stale artifacts. The
  canon vectors pin the hash across substrates. These caught me repeatedly
  today.
- **Cross-substrate CI passes**, with the private submodules initialised.
- **The published primitives are real code with green parity gates**, not
  vapour. That they have no users is a distribution fact, not a quality one.

## 6. The blocker, and why it is not the one I named

The chain looks like this:

```text
RFC tranche → ratification → propose → cowitness → verdict
                                 ↓
              packages/witness: "A co-signature requires the private key.
              You cannot sign as someone else … reach the threshold without
              that many real private keys — Sybil-resistant by construction"
                                 ↓
                    ~/.trinity/keys does not exist here
```

I have been calling the keys the critical path all session. **That is wrong, and
the repository already says so.**

`paper/CLAIMS.md`: _"All voice keys reside on one host today."_
`paper/REVIEW_NOTES.md`: _"all private keys reside on one host, so quorum
Sybil-resistance is 'governance discipline plus an audit trail, not a
cryptographic guarantee'."_

The default policy is **3-of-5 cowitness AYEs**. If all five private keys live
on one machine controlled by one person, a 3-of-5 quorum is one person signing
three times. Retrieving the keys from the mac-mini restores the _ability to
sign_. It does not create a quorum, because a quorum is a claim about **distinct
principals**, and there is one.

**So the bottleneck is not keys, not ratification, not code. It is that a
multi-voice governance substrate has one human.** Everything downstream —
ratification, the RFC set, the acceptance layer's credibility — inherits that.

This is measured, not inferred: 1388 of 1392 commits, 14 of 15 pull requests,
one author on `myc`, two on `omega` and `liquid` (the second being an automation
identity).

## 7. What is actually valuable here

Ranked by how much survives contact with someone outside.

1. **The acceptance-layer thinking.**
   `execution success ≠ validation success ≠
   acceptance`; suitability that
   cannot be self-reported; eligibility that replays separated from
   authorization that is attributed. `entire.io` raised $60M at $300M to build
   the observation layer and left this open, apparently because it conflicts
   with their product promise. This is the one idea here with a validated market
   and no incumbent.
2. **`warrant` and `sigma-glyph`.** Self-contained, three implementations, 47/47
   and 40/40 differential agreement, Lean proofs, adversarial review from three
   model families. More rigorous and more legible to an outsider than trinity
   itself.
3. **`oaip`.** A published spec with a normative dependency on `warrant`, and a
   scar in its own text about a runtime tag it recorded falsely — which is the
   most credible thing a spec can contain.
4. **The published primitives.** Real, tested, unused.
5. **The trinity ontology.** 872 chords, hex coordinates, octants, the
   living-substrate framing. Genuinely coherent, and the part least legible to
   anyone who did not build it.

The ordering is uncomfortable and it is what the measurements support: **the
most valuable assets are the ones outside `trinity/`.**

## 8. Options

**A. Continue building the substrate.** Compounds depth in a system whose
premise requires principals it does not have. Every added chord widens the
ledger-to-code ratio. Honest expected outcome: month four looks like month
three.

**B. Get one more human.** The only move that unblocks the governance layer at
its root. Everything else is downstream. Hardest, slowest, and the one with the
highest ceiling — a real second key-holder makes 3-of-5 mean something and makes
every ratified tranche real rather than ceremonial.

**C. Ship the differentiated idea where there is demand.** `oaip` + `warrant` as
the acceptance layer that `entire.io`-shaped tools lack. The bridge spec written
today (`probes/entire-oaip-bridge-v0`) is the concrete first step and it needs
no governance, no keys and no ratification. Also the most likely path to (B),
because people arrive through something they wanted to use.

**D. Freeze trinity as research, publish the ideas.** The RFC set, the paper,
the honesty machinery. Preserves the value, stops the compounding, costs the
identity of the project.

## 9. Recommendation

**C, then B. Stop A.**

The case: the problem this ecosystem works on was validated at $300M three
months after the repository was created, by a team that built the half requiring
capital and left open the half requiring taste. That is close to the best
possible market signal for a small project, and it is not being acted on.

The asset with the shortest path to an outside user is **not** trinity. It is
`oaip` + `warrant`: self-contained, multi-implementation, spec-first, and
directly complementary to a tool that now has real users. A bridge that turns an
Entire checkpoint into an accepted, signed decision is a demo a stranger can run
and a sentence a stranger can repeat.

Trinity should stop growing during that. Not be abandoned — the machinery is
genuinely good and several pieces (the canon vectors, the honesty guards, the
custody check) are worth keeping alive. But adding the 873rd chord to a ledger
with one author and zero readers has a measured return, and it is the trajectory
in §2.

**What would falsify this recommendation:**

- If a second key-holder appears, (B) becomes available directly and the
  governance layer stops being ceremonial — reorder immediately.
- If the bridge in `probes/entire-oaip-bridge-v0` fails its own falsifiers —
  particularly if `FilesTouched` does not resolve against `CommitSHA` — then (C)
  has no cheap first step and the ordering is wrong.
- If `entire.io` ships signing and an acceptance gate, the differentiation in
  §7.1 closes and (C) loses its opening.
- If the trinity ontology turns out to be load-bearing for `oaip`/`warrant` in
  some way this audit missed, then freezing it damages the thing being shipped.

## 10. What this audit does not cover

- No security review of the published primitives beyond their own tests.
- No assessment of `liquid`, `myc` or `omega` internals; only their commit
  activity and their gates as seen from `trinity`.
- No measure of whether the ontology is _correct_, only of whether it is
  _adopted_. Those are different questions and this audit answers the second.
- The interpretation in §2 (decline) and §6 (one human is the bottleneck) are
  readings of the data, not data. The numbers under them are the data.
